The Land Itself

Panj Aab — The Five Rivers

Punjab means “land of five rivers.” The Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, and Sutlej have shaped this region for millennia. Their waters nourished the Indus Valley civilizations and later the people of Punjab. Our Panj Aab designs flow like those rivers — continuous, life-giving, and foundational.

Birth of the Khalsa & the Misl Era

Chamkaur 1704

In December 1704, at the small fort of Chamkaur Sahib, Guru Gobind Singh Ji and a handful of Khalsa warriors faced a vastly larger Mughal army. The stand became legendary for the courage of the Sikhs who refused to surrender. It is remembered as a defining moment of the saint-soldier ethos — dignity and resolve in the face of overwhelming odds.

Saragarhi & the World Wars

Saragarhi 1897

On 12 September 1897, 21 soldiers of the 36th Sikhs Regiment held the small signaling post of Saragarhi against an estimated 10,000–12,000 Orakzai and Afridi tribesmen. All 21 fought to the last man. Their last stand is one of the most celebrated acts of collective bravery in military history. We honor them with solemnity and respect.

Khalsa Ideals

Deg Teg Fateh

“Deg Teg Fateh” is the Khalsa anthem: victory to the deg (the cauldron of langar and charity) and the teg (the sword that defends justice). It balances compassion with strength — the heart of the Sant Sipahi ideal.

The Gurus

Miri Piri

Miri (temporal power) and Piri (spiritual power) represent the dual sovereignty of the Sikh path. Guru Hargobind Ji formalized the concept by wearing two swords. A Sikh is both engaged in the world and sovereign within.

Birth of the Khalsa — Vaisakhi 1699

The Khanda & 1699

On Vaisakhi 1699 at Anandpur Sahib, Guru Gobind Singh Ji created the Khalsa. The Khanda emblem — double-edged sword, chakkar, and two kirpans — became its symbol. 1699 is the year the community of the pure was formally established.

Mai Bhago — The Women Warriors

Mai Bhago & Phulkari

Mai Bhago was a warrior who rallied the 40 Sikhs (later known as the Chali Mukte) who had earlier left the camp, leading them in battle at Muktsar. Phulkari is the traditional embroidery of Punjab — vibrant floral patterns worked by hand, often by women. We bring both together: strength and textile heritage.
