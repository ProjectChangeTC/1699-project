# AGENT HANDOFF — Punjabi Heritage Apparel Brand Website

You are building an e-commerce website for a new Punjabi heritage streetwear brand. This document is your complete brief. Follow it exactly; where it gives a hard rule (marked RULE), do not deviate.

---

## 1. MISSION

A clothing brand for the Punjabi generation (Gen Z and young diaspora) that wears Sikh history with pride. Every design is rooted in real heritage — the Khalsa warrior tradition, the Sikh Gurus, historic battles, the five rivers of Punjab, the regions of Punjab — and every product page tells the story behind the design, so the wearer learns the history. The brand presents Sikhism and the Sikh warrior ethos (Sant-Sipahi, saint-soldier) with dignity and historical accuracy, replacing stereotypes with education.

## 2. BRAND CONCEPT

- Heritage streetwear: modern oversized/streetwear silhouettes carrying centuries of Punjabi and Sikh history.
- Each product is a history lesson you can wear. The "Heritage" section of the site is as important as the shop.
- Tone: proud, educational, confident. Never preachy, never stereotyping, never sensationalist.

## 3. DESIGN DIRECTION

- Colors: deep kesari/saffron, Khalsa royal blue, black, cream/off-white, gold accents.
- Aesthetic: premium streetwear meets heritage — bold typography mixing Gurmukhi and English, minimal-but-powerful graphics for basics, maximalist epic illustrations for statement pieces, gold-foil and embroidery textures.
- Motifs: Khanda, Nishan Sahib colors, Phulkari embroidery patterns, Gurmukhi calligraphy, historic Punjabi art styles.
- Design bar: the best, most creative heritage designs ever put on clothing — full-back illustrations, flowing compositions that use the whole garment, embroidery, foil, sleeve prints. Nothing generic.

## 4. SITE STRUCTURE

1. Homepage — hero with brand statement, featured drops, heritage teaser.
2. Shop — product grid with filters (men / women / collection / garment type).
3. Heritage — the educational heart: short reads on the history behind each design (see section 7). Every product links to its story.
4. About — brand mission: wear the history, learn the history.
5. Standard e-commerce: cart, checkout, size guide, contact.

## 7. HERITAGE STORYTELLING FRAMEWORK

The brand tells one continuous story. Each era gives warriors to honor, words to print, and traditions to weave into designs.

1. **The land itself (ancient):** the five rivers and Indus Valley roots (Harappa). Design hook: river line-art, "Panj Aab."
2. **The Gurus (1469–1708):** from Guru Nanak Dev Ji to Guru Gobind Singh Ji. Design hook: "Ik Onkar ੴ", Gurmukhi calligraphy, dates.
3. **Birth of the Khalsa (Vaisakhi 1699):** the Panj Pyare, the Five Ks (Kesh, Kangha, Kara, Kachera, Kirpan). Design hook: "1699" pieces, Khanda.
4. **The Misl warriors (1700s):** the Dal Khalsa confederacies vs. the Mughal Empire and Afghan invasions — Baba Deep Singh, Jassa Singh Ahluwalia, Akali Phula Singh.
5. **Sher-e-Punjab (1799–1849):** Maharaja Ranjit Singh's Sikh Empire — generals Hari Singh Nalwa and Rani Jindan (warrior queen regent).
6. **Saragarhi & the World Wars (1897–1945):** the 21 of Saragarhi; Sikh service in both World Wars.
7. **Mai Bhago — the women warriors:** Mai Bhago leading the 40 (Chali Mukte) at Muktsar. Cornerstone of the women's line.
8. **Partition (1947):** the division of Punjab — trauma, uprooting, resilience. Remembrance with dignity, never despair.
9. **1984 — remembrance and resilience:** honor lives lost with solemnity. Remembrance and unbroken spirit — gravity, never sensationalism.
10. **The diaspora (today):** Sikhs carrying Punjab worldwide — Canada, UK, US. City-edition drops (Toronto, London, New York, Vancouver).
11. **Chardi Kala (always):** the eternal rising spirit — the brand's closing word on every collection.

- Langar and Seva — build a charity tie-in per drop.

- Phulkari embroidery, Punjabi folk patterns — textile heritage as print.
- Vaisakhi and Gurpurab drops — timed releases around Sikh occasions.

## 8. SENSITIVITY RULES (HARD — DO NOT BREAK)

- RULE: Never depict the Sikh Gurus' faces or figures on garments or site imagery. Use symbols (Khanda, Ik Onkar), Gurmukhi calligraphy, dates, and quotes instead.
- RULE: All religious references must be historically accurate and reverent. When in doubt, keep it symbolic.
- RULE: 1984 and Partition content is remembrance-only: solemn, dignified, never sensationalized or politicized.
- RULE: Depictions of warriors (Mai Bhago, generals, Khalsa soldiers) are artistic/historical illustration, not religious iconography — this is acceptable and encouraged.
